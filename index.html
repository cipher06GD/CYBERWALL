CYBER SAFETY & DIGITAL RESCUE GUIDE

1. How to Identify Phishing Emails & Social Engineering

  • Scrutinize Sender Addresses: Look closely at the domain after the "@" symbol. Scammers use lookalike domains (e.g., security@sb1-bank.net instead of official bank domains).
  • Manufactured Urgency: Phrases like "Account suspended in 24 hours" or "Act immediately" create panic so victims click without thinking.
  • The Golden Rule: Legitimate banks, schools, and organizations NEVER ask for OTPs, PINs, or account passwords via email, phone, or messaging apps.
  • Inspect Embedded Hyperlinks: Hover your mouse cursor over any link before clicking to view the actual destination address. If it shows an IP address or strange domain, do not click.
  • Generic Greetings: Phishing emails often start with generic salutations like "Dear Customer" or "Dear Lucky Winner" because they are sent out in bulk.

2. How to Create & Maintain Strong Passwords

  • Length Beats Complexity: A 14+ character password is exponentially harder for brute-force attacks to crack than an 8-character password.
  • Use the Passphrase Method: Combine 4 random, memorable words with symbols and numbers (e.g., Solar!Tiger#Mango49). It is easy to remember and nearly impossible to guess.
  • Never Reuse Passwords: If one website suffers a data breach, attackers use credential stuffing to test that same email and password across other accounts.
  • Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA/2FA): Enable 2FA on your email, banking, and social accounts. Even if your password is stolen, the attacker cannot log in without the secondary verification code.
  • Use a Password Manager: Store your unique, strong passwords in an encrypted password manager so you only need to remember one master password.

3. How to Detect Fake Websites & Malicious URLs

  • The HTTPS Myth: The padlock icon and https:// only mean your network connection is encrypted. Cybercriminals can easily get free SSL certificates for fake websites!
  • Typosquatting & Digit Swaps: Scammers register lookalike domains using digit-for-letter swaps (e.g., amaz0n.com, paypa1.com, g00gle.com).
  • Hyphenated Brand Names: Beware of domains combining brands with discount words (e.g., www.brand-mega-sale-deals.net).
  • IP-Address URLs: Legitimate public websites almost never use bare IP addresses (e.g., http://192.168.43.15/login.php) for public customer logins.
  • URL Shorteners: Services like bit.ly and tinyurl hide the real target destination. Preview them using link expansion tools before opening.

4. How to Avoid Online Shopping Scams & Payment Fraud

  • Unrealistic Discount Traps: Flagship smartphones or laptops advertised at 90% discount (e.g., Rs. 999) are guaranteed scams designed to steal money and card data.
  • UPI Fraud Rule: Entering your UPI PIN is ONLY done when sending money from your account. You NEVER need to enter your UPI PIN to receive money or cashbacks.
  • QR Code Scams: Never scan a QR code sent by an unknown buyer on classified sites. Scanning a QR code sends funds from your account.
  • Verify Payment Gateways: Ensure transactions redirect through recognized, regulated payment gateways (e.g., Razorpay, PayU, official bank gateways).
  • Delivery & COD Scams: Refuse cash-on-delivery parcels that you or your family members did not order.

5. Identity Theft Protection & PII Safety

  • What is PII? Personally Identifiable Information includes full legal name, date of birth, Aadhaar/national ID, residential address, mother's maiden name, and biometric data.
  • Masked Aadhaar: Download a Masked Aadhaar card from uidai.gov.in where the first 8 digits are hidden (showing only the last 4 digits). It is legally valid everywhere.
  • Lock Biometrics: Use the mAadhaar application or UIDAI portal to lock your biometric data when not in use to prevent unauthorized authentication.
  • Protect Security Questions: Avoid answering security questions with factual information that can be found on your social media (e.g., pet's name, school, mother's surname).
  • SIM Swap Defense: If your mobile phone suddenly loses network signal with "No Service" for hours, contact your telecom provider immediately to check for an unauthorized SIM swap attempt.

6. Emergency Cybercrime Reporting in India

  • National Cyber Crime Helpline: Call 1930 (Toll-Free, 24x7) immediately if you have been defrauded.
  • The Golden Hour: Reporting financial fraud to 1930 within the first 1 to 2 hours enables law enforcement and the Reserve Bank of India to freeze the money trail in the beneficiary account.
  • Online Cybercrime Portal: File formal complaints, upload evidence, and track FIR status at cybercrime.gov.in.
  • Preserve Digital Evidence: Save screenshots, transaction reference numbers, SMS alerts, payment receipts, email headers, and chat transcripts.
  • Bank Escalation: Immediately inform your bank's official fraud helpline to block your ATM/Debit card, UPI ID, and internet banking credentials.
